Advanced Gerunds & Infinitives

المصادر المتقدمة

Go far beyond "I like swimming." At C1, the same verb can mean completely different things depending on whether it takes a gerund or infinitive. Master meaning-change verbs, perfect forms (having done / to have done), passive forms (being done / to be done), and essential preposition + gerund patterns.

📖 Grammar 💬 Everyday English 📘 Reading ✅ Check It!
📋 Three Advanced Areas
ثلاثة مجالات متقدمة
🟡 Area 1
Meaning-Change Verbs
Same verb — completely different meaning depending on gerund or infinitive.
stop smoking ≠ stop to smoke
🟢 Area 2
Perfect Forms
having + PP / to have + PP
He denied having stolen it.
She seems to have left.
🟣 Area 3
Passive Forms
being + PP / to be + PP
She hates being interrupted.
He expects to be promoted.
🟡 Area 1 — Meaning-Change Verbs
أفعال تغيّر معناها تبعاً للصيغة
Some verbs take both gerund and infinitive — but the meaning changes completely.
+gerund (-ing) = one meaning  |  +infinitive (to…) = different meaning
stop
+ gerund (-ing)
= quit / cease doing
He stopped smoking.(= he quit) 🔉
+ infinitive (to…)
= pause in order to do
He stopped to smoke.(= paused and lit a cigarette) 🔉
remember
+ gerund (-ing)
= recall a past action
I remember meeting her.(= I recall it happened) 🔉
+ infinitive (to…)
= don't forget to do
Remember to call him.(= don't forget in future) 🔉
forget
+ gerund (-ing)
= forget that something happened
I'll never forget visiting Rome.(= won't forget the memory) 🔉
+ infinitive (to…)
= fail to do something
Don't forget to lock the door.(= do it!) 🔉
regret
+ gerund (-ing)
= feel sorry about a past action
I regret quitting my job.(= I'm sorry I did it) 🔉
+ infinitive (to…)
= formal sorry for bad news
We regret to inform you.(formal announcement) 🔉
try
+ gerund (-ing)
= experiment / do as a test
Try adding more salt.(= experiment) 🔉
+ infinitive (to…)
= make an effort / attempt
He tried to lift the box.(= he attempted but it was hard) 🔉
mean
+ gerund (-ing)
= involve / entail
This means working late.(= it involves/entails it) 🔉
+ infinitive (to…)
= intend to do
I meant to call you.(= I intended to) 🔉
go on
+ gerund (-ing)
= continue the same action
She went on talking.(= continued the same thing) 🔉
+ infinitive (to…)
= move on to next action
He went on to become famous.(= moved to next stage) 🔉
need
+ gerund (-ing)
= require (passive meaning)
The car needs fixing.(= needs to be fixed) 🔉
+ infinitive (to…)
= have to / must do
You need to fix it.(= you must do it) 🔉
🟢 Area 2 — Perfect Gerund & Perfect Infinitive
صيغة الماضي التام مع المصدر والـ ing
Use these to show an action that happened before the main verb — adding a time dimension.
having + PP
Perfect Gerund — shows the -ing action happened before the main verb.
Common after: deny / admit / recall / apologise for / be accused of

"He denied having stolen the money." 🔉
"She admitted having made a mistake."
"I recall having seen that film before."
to have + PP
Perfect Infinitive — shows the infinitive action happened before the main verb.
Common after: seem / appear / claim / believe / be said

"She seems to have forgotten about the meeting." 🔉
"He is said to have left the country."
"They claim to have discovered a new species."
🟣 Area 3 — Passive Gerund & Passive Infinitive
صيغة المبني للمجهول مع المصدر والـ ing
When the subject receives the action (is done to, not does), use passive gerund or passive infinitive.
being + PP
Passive Gerund — used where a gerund would normally appear.
"She hates being interrupted." 🔉
"He avoids being seen in public."
"Children love being praised."
"The manager disliked being challenged."
to be + PP
Passive Infinitive — used where an infinitive would normally appear.
"He expects to be promoted next year." 🔉
"She hopes to be selected for the team."
"They deserve to be recognised."
"The report needs to be submitted by Friday."
🔗 Preposition + Gerund Patterns
حرف الجر + الـ ing — قاعدة ثابتة
After a preposition, always use a gerund (not an infinitive).
• interested in + doing  • without + doing  • instead of + doing
interested in doing
"She is interested in studying abroad." 🔉
capable of doing
"He is capable of achieving great things." 🔉
responsible for doing
"She is responsible for managing the team." 🔉
without doing
"He left without saying a word." 🔉
instead of doing
"Instead of complaining, try to find a solution." 🔉
in addition to doing
"In addition to speaking French, she speaks Arabic." 🔉
keen on doing
"He is keen on learning new skills." 🔉
accustomed to doing
"She is accustomed to working under pressure." 🔉
tired of doing
"I'm tired of waiting for results." 🔉
📌 Fixed Expressions + Gerund
تعبيرات ثابتة مع الـ ing
ExpressionExample
can't help doing 🔉"I can't help laughing when he tells stories."
can't stand doing 🔉"She can't stand being ignored in meetings."
it's worth doing 🔉"It's worth reading the contract carefully."
it's no use doing 🔉"It's no use arguing — he won't change his mind."
be used to doing 🔉"She is used to working long hours."
get used to doing 🔉"He got used to commuting by train."
look forward to doing 🔉"I look forward to hearing from you."
there's no point doing 🔉"There's no point waiting — he's not coming."
⚠ Common Mistakes — أخطاء شائعة
❌
I look forward to hear from you. → I look forward to hearing from you.
بعد "to" الحرف (وليس حرف المصدر): استخدم الـ gerund وليس base verb
❌
He stopped to smoke — so now he's healthy. → He stopped smoking — so now he's healthy.
stopped to smoke = توقف ليدخّن (لم يقلع!) / stopped smoking = أقلع عن التدخين
❌
She was accused of to steal the documents. → She was accused of stealing the documents.
بعد حروف الجر دائماً gerund (-ing) — وليس infinitive
❌
He seems to forgot about the appointment. → He seems to have forgotten about the appointment.
لحدث قبل الفعل الرئيسي: to have + past participle (perfect infinitive)
❌
The report needs to be submitting by Friday. → The report needs to be submitted by Friday.
passive infinitive: to be + past participle (وليس to be + gerund)
💡 Quick Tips
Prepositions
After any preposition → always gerund: in doing / without saying / instead of going 🔉بعد أي حرف جر دائماً الـ ing — هذه قاعدة بلا استثناء
to = preposition
look forward to / be used to / get used to — "to" here is a preposition, so use gerund: to hearing / to working 🔉"to" في هذه التعبيرات حرف جر — ليس حرف مصدر — فيأتي بعده gerund
Perfect forms
having done (gerund context) and to have done (infinitive context) both show the action happened before the main verb 🔉كلا الصيغتين تدلان على أن الفعل حدث قبل الفعل الرئيسي
need
"The car needs fixing" (thing needs to be fixed) vs "You need to fix it" (person must act) 🔉need + gerund: الشيء يحتاج للإصلاح / need + infinitive: الشخص يجب أن يفعل

📘 The Art of Mastery
فن الإتقان

Mastering a skill means committing to a long, often uncomfortable process. Researchers studying expertise consistently find that those who succeed are capable of enduring prolonged discomfort without abandoning their goals. 🔉

Many beginners make the mistake of stopping to check their progress too frequently. They forget that progress in the early stages is often invisible. Instead of measuring tiny gains, experts recommend simply going on practising with deliberate focus. 🔉

Having studied thousands of learners, psychologist K. Anders Ericsson found that truly skilled individuals are accustomed to receiving critical feedback without being discouraged by it. They welcome being challenged because they understand that difficulty signals growth. 🔉

One common misunderstanding is that talent means not needing to try. In reality, even the most gifted musicians admit having struggled enormously in the early years. They tried changing their approach repeatedly until something clicked. 🔉

It is also worth remembering that mastery requires being patient with yourself. There is no point rushing — the brain needs time to consolidate new skills. Experts seem to have internalised this truth instinctively. 🔉
